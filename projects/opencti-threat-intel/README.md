# OpenCTI Threat Intelligence Platform Lab

**Track:** Threat Intelligence  
**Status:** Not started — starter guide only  
**Estimated time:** 2–4 hours  
**Difficulty:** Beginner–intermediate

## Objective

OpenCTI Threat Intelligence Platform Lab is a hands-on learning exercise designed to build a defensible, evidence-based workflow. Your goal is to explain what you did, support conclusions with your own evidence, and identify what the sample cannot prove.

## Safety and scope

> Use public demo or mock STIX bundle; local deployment may be resource-intensive.


## Safe lab setup

Use synthetic, benign, local, or explicitly authorized material only. Stop if scope is unclear. Preserve originals and work from copies. Sanitize evidence. Never commit secrets, personal data, or confidential employer/client information.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. **Define scope:** Record objective, in-scope material, exclusions, and investigation question.
2. **Prepare sample:** Record provenance, date/time (UTC), tool/version, and sample hash where relevant.
3. **Establish baseline:** Note expected behavior, key fields, assets, identities, and known limitations.
4. **Perform exercise:** Follow the project-specific steps below; record question, action, result, and interpretation.
5. **Validate:** Consider a benign explanation, cross-check key observations, and separate facts from assumptions.
6. **Report:** Summarize evidence, findings, confidence/severity, recommendations, and limitations.
7. **Review:** Sanitize screenshots and ensure every claim is supported by your own work.

## Project-specific exercise

Inspect report/actor/malware/indicator/relationship/marking objects; trace relationship graph; record confidence/validity/provenance; export sanitized notes; explain defensive use.

## Practice questions

- What evidence directly answers the investigation question?
- What alternative benign explanation did you consider?
- Which statements are facts, interpretations, or unknowns?
- What is the impact and confidence of each finding?
- What would you do next, and how would you verify it?

## Evidence checklist

- [ ] Scope, objective, and safety boundary recorded.
- [ ] Sample provenance, date/time, and tools/versions recorded.
- [ ] Screenshots of your own steps saved and sanitized.
- [ ] Queries, commands, code, or configuration excerpts saved as text.
- [ ] Important outputs captured with context and timestamps.
- [ ] Findings supported by evidence; facts separated from assumptions.
- [ ] Recommendations and validation steps documented.
- [ ] Limitations and false-positive considerations included.
- [ ] Final report reviewed for accuracy and privacy.

## Final report structure

1. Executive summary
2. Scope, environment, and exclusions
3. Method and tools actually used
4. Evidence and observations (reference evidence filenames)
5. Findings, impact, confidence, and severity rationale
6. Recommendations and validation plan
7. Limitations and lessons learned

## Completion gate

This is a **starter guide**, not proof of completion. Keep status **Not started** or **In progress** until you personally perform the lab. Mark **Completed** only after your real screenshots, outputs, report, limitations, and recommendations are saved and reviewed. Never invent results or claim tools you did not use.
